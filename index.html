Ford Bronco Roll Cage Accessories — What Works

The 2021–2026 Ford Bronco full-size has a genuine exposed roll cage — A-pillars, B-pillars, overhead bars, rear sport bars.

Updated: August 2026  ·  2021–2026 Ford Bronco full-size only  ·  Not Bronco Sport

The Ford Bronco's roll cage is one of the defining features of the vehicle. It's not a cosmetic bar welded on for looks — it's a structural steel cage that runs A-pillar to rear sport bar and is fully exposed on the interior. Every bar is a potential mounting point, every span between bars is potential storage, and every exposed section at head height is a potential injury hazard if you don't address it.

This guide covers four main categories of roll cage accessories for the full-size Bronco and points you to the two brands doing it best for grab handles — which is where the most money gets spent and where the quality delta is biggest.

Full-size Bronco only. This entire site covers the 2021–2026 Ford Bronco full-size (2-door and 4-door). The Bronco Sport has no exposed roll cage. If you're searching for Bronco Sport interior accessories, you're in the wrong place.

The Bronco's Cage — Four Areas to Work With

A-Pillar Bars

Front grab points. Primary location for aftermarket grab handles. Exposed at shoulder and head height for front passengers and rear occupants. Factory plastic handles here are minimal.

B-Pillar Bars

Mid-cab grab points. Critical on a 4-door for rear seat passengers. Also at head height — foam padding here matters more than most owners realize until they need it.

Overhead Bars

Above the rear seats. Mostly ignored. An overhead MOLLE panel bolted here changes your whole gear organization approach — dead space becomes organized storage.

Rear Sport Bars

Behind the second row. Chase rack attachment point. Carry a Hi-Lift, shovel, spare, or fuel can. Most chase rack options clamp without drilling — verify tube diameter.
